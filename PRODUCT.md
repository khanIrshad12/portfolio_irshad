# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary audience: HR recruiters and hiring screeners who need to quickly assess role fit, credibility, and next steps. Secondary audiences include technical interviewers and tech leads evaluating shipped proof, plus clients or project buyers looking for freelance or contract work.

## Product Purpose

A personal resume and portfolio website that gets recruiters to shortlist and contact the candidate for interviews. Success means a fast scan that confirms role fit, surfaces real shipped work (experience projects and GitHub), and makes contact or resume download effortless.

## Positioning

A neo-brutalist, animation-forward portfolio that proves I can ship.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Contact / email me
- Secondary CTA: View or download resume
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: A neo-brutalist, animation-forward portfolio that proves I can ship.
- Belief ladder: Role fit → Real shipped proof → Clear, credible next step (contact)
- Proof on hand: Resume, experience projects, GitHub personal projects, LinkedIn profile

## Brand Personality

Bold, unapologetic, meticulous. The site should feel confident and direct — not polished-corporate — with craft visible in every interaction and animation.

## Anti-references

Too corporate, overly clean consulting style. Generic modern portfolio templates, SaaS landing-page clichés, glassmorphism, gradient text, and timid neutral palettes that read as "safe AI portfolio."

## Design Principles

- Show shipped proof before asking for trust — projects and code links are the evidence.
- Every fold earns the next scroll; animation supports hierarchy, not decoration for its own sake.
- Recruiter speed wins: scannable structure, clear role fit, one obvious contact path.
- Bold visual identity as a credibility signal — the design itself demonstrates frontend craft.
- Admin customization without breaking the brutalist system — theme tokens, not free-form chaos.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline: sufficient contrast on body text and interactive elements, full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, and a `prefers-reduced-motion` mode that replaces choreographed motion with instant or crossfade transitions.
